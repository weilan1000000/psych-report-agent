# Writing Principles

## Audience

The report is written for parents and may also be read by teachers. It is not written for clinicians, researchers, or the child directly.

Parents should finish reading with three responses:

- "This is my child."
- "I understand."
- "I know what to do."

## Three Quality Criteria

### 1. Data portrait matches the prose

Every sentence should be traceable to data.

- Strengths in the core portrait must correspond to high-score or high-level dimensions.
- Developing areas must correspond to low-score, lower-level, or developing dimensions.
- Do not write a core portrait that contradicts later indicators.
- Do not use a general template when the child's actual profile points elsewhere.

### 2. Score band matches wording strength

Every selected dimension must be calibrated before writing.

- 优势区: write as a clear advantage, but avoid inflated or fixed labels.
- 常态区: write as stable, ordinary, or balanced. Do not praise it as exceptional and do not describe it as a serious weakness.
- 潜力区: write as an area for gradual development. Do not exaggerate it into a strength and do not make the child sound deficient.

Use the same calibration in the core growth portrait and detailed indicator analysis. If a dimension is 常态区 in the data, it cannot become an outstanding strength in the prose.

### 3. Warm but not frightening

Low scores must be framed as developmental information.

Use:

- "正在发展中"
- "目前表现为"
- "在某些情境下可能需要更多支持"
- "可以通过...逐步练习"

Avoid:

- "差"
- "弱"
- "问题"
- "冲动"
- "懒惰"
- "永远改不了"
- "天生如此"

### 4. Suggestions are specific and actionable

Each suggestion must map to a data feature.

Each suggestion should include:

- a scene: homework, exploration, parent-child communication, classroom, reading, play, or daily routine
- an action: what the parent can do
- wording: what the parent can say
- a first step that can be tried today

Avoid abstract advice such as "建立契约", "刻意练习", "培养元认知", "保持积极心态", or "多鼓励".

## One-Vote-Fail Rules

The report fails if any of these appear:

- clinical or diagnostic labels, such as "智力低下", "多动症倾向", "自闭特质", "情绪障碍"
- fatalistic conclusions, such as "天生就这样", "这辈子改不了", "注定学不好"
- blame toward parents, family, teachers, or environment without evidence
-人格 insult or moral judgment, such as "懒惰", "狡猾", "内心阴暗", "品行不端"
- uncontrolled absolute wording, such as repeated "极其", "完美", "顶尖", "无懈可击"
- severe internal contradiction between core portrait and detailed indicators
- score-band contradiction, such as 潜力区 written as excellent, 常态区 written as either outstanding or poor, or 优势区 written as only average
- advice that has no visible connection to the child's strengths, developing areas, or indicator pattern

## Data Sketch Before Writing

Before writing, create a short internal list:

- top 30% dimensions: candidates for strengths
- bottom 30% dimensions: candidates for developing areas
- middle dimensions: usually not in the core portrait
- contradictory-looking dimensions: explain how they can coexist
- score bands or level labels for each selected dimension

The core portrait should focus on the strongest strengths and the most important developing areas.
